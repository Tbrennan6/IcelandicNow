Now that you are familiar with weak nouns and grammatical gender, it is time to put your knowledge into action. 

## Inflection Table
|                | **Masculine** | **Feminine** | **Neuter** |
|----------------|---------------|--------------|------------|
| **Nominative** | hann          | hún          | það        |
| **Accusative** | hann          | hana         | það        |
| **Dative**     | honum         | henni        | því        |
| **Genitive**   | hans          | hennar       | þess       |

Above, you can see the inflection table for the personal pronouns in the singular. While you work through the rest of the lesson, please do refer back to the table.

## Using Pronouns 
It is extremely common to use pronouns during conversations after a noun has been mentioned previously and it is clear based on context what is being refered to. 

**Conversation 1:**\
Person 1: Hvar er **hann**? - (Where is he?)\
Person 2: Hver? - (Who?)\
Person 1: **Siggi**. Hvar er hann? - (Siggi. Where is he?)

**Conversation 2:**\
Person 1: Er þetta danska? - (Is that Danish?)\
Person 2: Nei, þetta er íslenska. - (No, it's Icelandic.)\
Person 1: Talarðu **íslensku**? - (Do you speak Icelandic?)\
Person 2: Nei, ég tala **hana** ekki. - (No, I don't speak it.)

*Tip: All languages in Icelandic are weak feminine words with rare exceptions (e.g., enska, sænska, franska).*

**Conversation 3:**\
Person 1: Talar **pabbi** **íslensku**?
Person 2: Nei, **hann** talar **hana** ekki.
