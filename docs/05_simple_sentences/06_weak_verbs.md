So far, we have been exposed to a few different verbs (e.g., að elska, að tala, að heita) and have perhaps started to pick up on how they behave. In this lesson, we are going to drill down a little harder on verb inflections by learning how to inflect weak verbs in both the past and the present!

## What Are Weak Verbs?
Previously, we learned a little bit about weak nouns: a certain subset of all nouns that are categorized together because they behave the same way. Weak verbs are the same way!

Much like how weak nouns can be identified by their endings, weak verbs can be as well. They are the verbs that form their simple past tense by adding a /d/, /t/, or /ð/ sound. In English, every verb that adds *-ed* to form the simple past tense is weak.

*Tip: They developed during Proto-Germanic, later evolutionarily than strong verbs, and are much more numerous.*

Example 1: Play -> Played\
Example 2: Stay -> Stayed\
Example 3: Kill -> Killed\

This course will classify Icelandic weak verbs into two categories by their weak past endings: -aði verbs and -d/t/ð verbs. 

## -aði Verbs 
| **Pres. -aði** | **Sing.** | **Plur.** |
|----------------|-----------|-----------|
| **1st.**       | -a        | -um       |
| **2nd.**       | -ar       | -ið       |
| **3rd.**       | -ar       | -a        |

| **Past -aði** | **Sing.** | **Plur.** |
|---------------|-----------|-----------|
| **1st.**      | -aði      | -uðum     |
| **2nd.**      | -aðir     | -uðuð     |
| **3rd.**      | -aði      | -uðu      |

The *-aði* pattern is extremely common. In addition, it can be thought of as the default pattern because new words entering Icelandic often become *-aði* verbs. For example, the verb *to google* did not exist in any language before Google existed. When to google was created in Icelandic, it became an *-aði* verb. 

Example 1: Ég tala -> Ég talaði
Example 2: Þú talar -> Þú talaðir
Example 3: Hann talar -> Hann talaði
Example 4: Við tölum -> Við töluðum
Example 5: Þið talið -> Þið töluðuð
Example 6: Þeir tala -> Þeir töluðu

If this is the first time you are seeing these two charts, it may seem like a lot of information to take in--and it is. While many courses and textbooks prefer to teach only one tense at a time, we are going to take a different approach, and for two main reasons. 

1. The past and present tense are both extremely common, so knowing only one is extremely limiting. 
2. Seeing and learning both tenses together helps us to internalize the changes between the present and past tenses better!
