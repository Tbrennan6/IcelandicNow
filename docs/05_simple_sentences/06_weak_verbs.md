So far, we have been exposed to a few different verbs (e.g., að elska, að tala, að heita) and have perhaps started to pick up on how they behave. In this lesson, we are going to drill down a little harder on verb inflections by learning how to inflect weak verbs in both the past and the present!

## What Are Weak Verbs?
Previously, we learned a little bit about weak nouns: a certain subset of all nouns that are categorized together because they behave the same way. Weak verbs are the same way!

Much like how weak nouns can be identified by their endings, weak verbs can be as well. They are the verbs that form their simple past tense by adding a /d/, /t/, or /ð/ sound. In English, every verb that adds *-ed* to form the simple past tense is weak.

*Tip: They developed during Proto-Germanic, later evolutionarily than strong verbs, and are much more numerous.*

Example 1: Play -> Played\
Example 2: Stay -> Stayed\
Example 3: Kill -> Killed

This course will classify Icelandic weak verbs into two categories by their weak past endings: *-aði* verbs and *-di*/*-ti*/*-ði* verbs. 

## -aði Verbs 
| **Pres. -aði** | **Sing.** | **Plur.** |
|----------------|-----------|-----------|
| **1st.**       | -a        | -um       |
| **2nd.**       | -ar       | -ið       |
| **3rd.**       | -ar       | -a        |

| **Past -aði** | **Sing.** | **Plur.** |
|---------------|-----------|-----------|
| **1st.**      | -aði      | -uðum     |
| **2nd.**      | -aðir     | -uðuð     |
| **3rd.**      | -aði      | -uðu      |

The *-aði* pattern is extremely common. In addition, it can be thought of as the default pattern because new words entering Icelandic often become *-aði* verbs. For example, the verb *to google* did not exist in any language before Google existed. When to google was created in Icelandic, it became an *-aði* verb. 

Example 1: Ég tala -> Ég talaði\
Example 2: Þú talar -> Þú talaðir\
Example 3: Hann talar -> Hann talaði\
Example 4: Við tölum -> Við töluðum\
Example 5: Þið talið -> Þið töluðuð\
Example 6: Þeir tala -> Þeir töluðu

If this is the first time you are seeing these two charts, it may seem like a lot of information to take in--and it is. While many courses and textbooks prefer to teach only one tense at a time, we are going to take a different approach, and for two main reasons. 

1. The past and present tense are both extremely common, so knowing only one is extremely limiting. 
2. Seeing and learning both tenses together helps us to internalize the changes between the present and past tenses better!

## -di, -ti, and -ði
| **Pres. -Xi** | **Sing.** | **Plur.** |
|---------------|-----------|-----------|
| **1st.**      | -i        | -um       |
| **2nd.**      | -ir       | -ið       |
| **3rd.**      | -ir       | -a        |

| **Past -Xi** | **Sing.** | **Plur.** |
|--------------|-----------|-----------|
| **1st.**     | -Xi       | -Xum      |
| **2nd.**     | -Xir      | -Xuð      |
| **3rd.**     | -Xi       | -Xu       |

The *-di*,*-ti*, *-ði* conjugation patterns can be seen above in two tables, one for the present and one for the past.

In the present tense, these three verb types inflect the exact same, while in the past tense, they differ by a single letter. In the chart, this differing letter is represented by the letter *x* in the table above. You can match the letter *x* in the table with the letter in the name of the verb type to find that verb's simple past endings.

For example, the *-di* verbs end with *-dir* in the second-person singular in the past.

There is no practical or deducible reason to explain why some weak verbs are assigned to be  *-di*, *-ti*, *-ði* verbs instead of *-aði* verbs, but once a verb is a *-di*, *-ti*, *-ði* verb, which of the three ending patterns it will follow is based on what letter or combination of letters ends the verb's stem.

Tip: Remember, the verb stem is the part of the verb that comes before the inflectional ending is added.

The rule is as follows:

B/Ð/L/M/N -> -di\
F/G/Gj/R/(vowel) -> -ði\
D/K/Kj/P/S/T -> -ti

Example 1: Ég reyni -> Ég reyndi
Example 2: Þú reynir -> Þú reyndir
Example 3: Hún reynir -> Hún reyndi
Example 4: Við reynum -> Við reyndum
Example 5: Þið reynið -> Þið reyndið
Example 6: Þær reyna -> Þær reyndu

Reyna -> Reyndi\
Trúa-> Trúði\
Breyta -> Breytti

*Tip: With time, the assignment of "-di", "-ti", and "-ði" verb endings to certain stem endings will become automatic as the sound combinations created by them become more familiar to you. 

*Note: Just because a verb's stem ending fits in one of the "-di", "-ti", and "-ði" endings, that does not automatically mean it is a "-di", "-ti", and "-ði"  verb. The verb could still be an "-aði" verb.*
